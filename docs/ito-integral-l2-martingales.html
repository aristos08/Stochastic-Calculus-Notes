Chapter 4
Construction of Itô's Integral with Respect to Continuous $L^2$-Bounded Martingales

Let \(S\leq T\) be stopping times and let \(Z\) be bounded and \(\F_S\)-measurable. Consider the elementary predictable process \[ H_s=Z\mathbf 1_{(S,T]}(s). \] For a continuous, \(L^2\)-bounded martingale \(M\), define \[ \int_0^tH_s\,dM_s :=Z\bigl(M_{t\wedge T}-M_{t\wedge S}\bigr). \] We also write this stochastic integral as \((H\cdot M)_t\).

Proposition 4.1.

The process \[ (H\cdot M)_t=Z\bigl(M_{t\wedge T}-M_{t\wedge S}\bigr) \] is an adapted, \(L^2\)-bounded martingale.

Proof.

The process is adapted: before \(S\) it is zero, while for \(t\geq S\), \(Z\) is \(\F_S\subseteq\F_t\)-measurable.

For \(L^2\)-boundedness, conditional Jensen and optional sampling give \[ \|M_{t\wedge T}\|_2\leq\|M_t\|_2, \qquad \|M_{t\wedge S}\|_2\leq\|M_t\|_2. \] Thus \[ \|(H\cdot M)_t\|_2 \leq2\|Z\|_\infty\|M_t\|_2, \] so \(H\cdot M\) is \(L^2\)-bounded.

It remains to prove the martingale property. Let \(R\) be an arbitrary bounded stopping time. For \(A\in\F_S\), define \[ S_A:=S\mathbf 1_A+\infty\mathbf 1_{A^c}, \qquad T_A:=T\mathbf 1_A+\infty\mathbf 1_{A^c}. \] Both are stopping times, and optional sampling applied to the bounded stopping times \(S_A\wedge R\leq T_A\wedge R\) gives \[ \E[M_{T_A\wedge R}]=\E[M_{S_A\wedge R}]. \] Outside \(A\), both stopped variables equal \(M_R\); hence \[ \E\bigl[(M_{T\wedge R}-M_{S\wedge R});A\bigr]=0. \] Since this holds for every \(A\in\F_S\), \[ \E[M_{T\wedge R}-M_{S\wedge R}\mid\F_S]=0. \] Multiplying by the bounded \(\F_S\)-measurable variable \(Z\), then taking expectations, gives \(\E[(H\cdot M)_R]=0=(H\cdot M)_0\). The converse optional-sampling criterion now implies that \(H\cdot M\) is a martingale.

∎

For more general $H$, we can take linear combinations of the earlier form: \[ H = \sum_{i=1}^n Z_{i-1} \mathbf{1}_{(T_{i-1}, T_i]}, \] where $0 \le T_0 < \cdots < T_n$ are stopping times. We will then define \[ (H \cdot M)_t = \sum Z_{i-1}(M_{T_i \wedge t} - M_{T_{i-1} \wedge t}). \]

For the purpose of (2), we will use the fact that an $L^2$-bounded martingale can be identified with elements in $L^2(\F_\infty)$. Recall that by the martingale convergence theorem, if $\{M_t\}$ is $L^2$-bounded, then $M_t \overset{L^2}{\to} M_\infty \in L^2(\F_\infty)$. Then, $M_t = \E(M_\infty \mid \F_t)$.

Thus, we have a one-to-one relation \[ \{M_t\}_{t \ge 0} \longleftrightarrow M_\infty \in L^2(\F_\infty). \]

For (1), note that \begin{align*} \E((H \cdot M)_\infty^2) &= \E\left(\left(\sum Z_{i-1}(M_{T_i} - M_{T_{i-1}})\right)^2\right) \\ &= \E \left( \sum Z_{i-1}^2 (M_{T_i}-M_{T_{i-1}})^2\right) \end{align*} Since $M_t^2 - [M]_t$ is a uniformly integrable martingale, for stopping times $S \le T$, we get \begin{align*} \E((M_T-M_S)^2 \mid \F_S) &= \E[M_T^2-M_S^2 \mid \F_S] \\ &= \E([M]_T - [M]_S \mid \F_S) \end{align*} Thus, \begin{align*} \E((H \cdot M)_\infty^2) &= \E\left(\sum Z_{i-1}^2([M]_{T_i} - [M]_{T_{i-1}})\right) \\ &= \E \left(\int_0^\infty H_s^2 d[M]_s \right) \end{align*} We will then define \[ \|H\|_M = \E \left(\int_0^\infty H_s^2 d[M]_s \right)^{1/2} \]

For (3), let $b\mcal{E}$ be the set of all processes $H$ with the form \[ H = \sum_{i=1}^n Z_{i-1} \mathbf{1}_{(T_{i-1}, T_{i}]}, \quad Z_i \in \F_{T_i} \] the b stands for bounded, the E for elementary.

Definition 4.2.

The $\sigma$-algebra generated by $b\mcal{E}$ is \[ \mcal{P} = \sigma(b \mcal{E}), \] which is called the predictable/previsible $\sigma$-algebra.

A process is called predictable/previsible if it is a $\mcal{P}$-measurable mapping from $[0, \infty) \times \Omega \to (\R, \mcal{B}(\R))$.

What processes are predictable? By monotone class theorem, all the Càglàd (continuous from the left with right limits existing) functions (in our case, Stochastic processes) are.

Define \[ L^2(M) = \left\{\text{predictable processes } H \text{ such that } \|H\|_M < \infty \right\}. \] We have defined $H \cdot M$ for $H \in b\mcal{E}$. Our goal is to show that $\overline{b \mcal{E}} = L^2(M)$, where the closure is taken under the earlier norm. The proof is omitted. (The idea is to use a version of the monotone class theorem.)

For (4), we have seen that \[ \E((H \cdot M)_\infty^2) = \E \left(\int_0^\infty H_s^2 d[M]_s \right) \] One side is $\|(H \cdot M)_\infty\|_2^2$, and the other is $\|H\|_M^2$. This means that the mapping $I \colon b\mcal{E} \cap L^2(M) \to L^2(\F_\infty)$ defined by $I(H) = (H \cdot M)_\infty$ is an isometry. Since $b\mcal{E}$ is dense in $L^2(M)$, this isometry extends uniquely to the closure $\overline{b \mcal{E}} = L^2(M)$.
